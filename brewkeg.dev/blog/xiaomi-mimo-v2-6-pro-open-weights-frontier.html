Xiaomi just open sourced a model that lands in the same tier as Claude Opus 5

MiMo V2.6 Pro scored 46 on the AA Intelligence Index, runs at 125 tokens a second, and costs $0.87 per million output tokens under an MIT license. The frontier is wider than you think.

2026-09-22·#open-weights#benchmarks#xiaomi#mimo

If you only follow the US labs, you probably missed this one. Yesterday, Xiaomi released MiMo V2.6 Pro under an MIT license. It is a one trillion parameter mixture of experts model with forty two billion active parameters per forward pass. It scores a 46 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, putting it in the same tier as Grok 4.7 and not far behind Claude Opus 5 or GPT 5.6 Sol. The pricing is $0.43 per million input tokens and $0.87 per million output tokens. That is roughly one twentieth to one sixtieth of what the closed frontier labs charge for comparable reasoning.

There is no hype in that sentence. Every number above is verifiable on the Artificial Analysis model page, which has been quietly tracking MiMo V2.6 Pro since the weights dropped on September 21.

Why this matters for the bill at the end of the month

Most of the discourse about model prices gets stuck on two numbers: input per million and output per million. That is fine for marketing decks. For anyone running real workloads, the more important question is what the inference actually costs end to end, including the tokens the model spends reasoning before it gives you an answer.

On that metric, MiMo V2.6 Pro is doing something genuinely interesting. vLLM reported running full parameter reinforcement learning on the 310 billion parameter checkpoint across more than a thousand TPUs, with bitwise matched rollouts against the trainer and all 310 billion parameters moving across the ICI fabric in under two seconds. That is not the kind of thing a lab can do if the weights are a marketing artifact. The training infrastructure behind the release looks serious.

If you are a developer in Lagos, Abuja, or Port Harcourt, this is the part that should make you sit up. The same Xiaomi API that powers the AA benchmark page is available right now. You can hit it from brewkeg the same way you hit Claude or GPT, and the bill per million output tokens is what brewkeg will charge you for it, in Naira, with no markup on the inference and no subscription expiring at the end of the month.

The benchmark number, in context

A 46 on the Intelligence Index does not mean MiMo V2.6 Pro is the smartest model you can run. Claude Opus 5 sits higher, as does the latest from OpenAI. What a 46 does mean is that MiMo has crossed the threshold where the answer to the question "is this model good enough to ship my product on" stops being "no" and starts being "depends on the workload."

For tasks that are mostly comprehension, summarization, structured extraction, code review, and the kind of agent work that lives or dies on tool calling reliability, MiMo V2.6 Pro is in the conversation. For tasks where you specifically need the absolute top of the leaderboard on a benchmark like SciCode or CritPt, you still want Claude Opus 5 or GPT 5.6 Sol and you should pay for them.

The interesting bit is the price gap. A reasoning call that costs you around a dollar on Opus 5 costs you roughly two cents on MiMo V2.6 Pro. For batch jobs, evaluation harnesses, or anything that processes a lot of text in the background, that gap is the difference between a feature you can ship and a feature that breaks your unit economics.

What "open weights under MIT" actually buys you

There is a real difference between "we published a research paper about the model" and "we released the weights under a license that lets you ship product on them." MiMo V2.6 Pro is the second kind. The MIT license means you can:

  • run the model yourself on your own hardware
  • fine tune it for your domain and ship the fine tune
  • host a competing API backed by the same weights
  • use it in commercial software without paying anyone

For brewkeg specifically, the practical consequence is that if Xiaomi's hosted API has an outage, an availability gap, or a price hike, the same model is downloadable from Hugging Face and self hostable. That is not true for Claude Opus 5. It is not true for GPT 5.6 Sol. It is not true for the Chinese closed weights from Z.ai or Moonshot. MiMo V2.6 Pro is one of the very few models in the 40 plus tier on the Intelligence Index that comes with that escape hatch.

What we are doing about it

MiMo V2.6 Pro is not yet on brewkeg as a routing target. We are running our own evaluation pass against the model this week, focused on the things our users actually hit us about: tool calling reliability on long agent traces, code completion on the languages Nigerian developers use most, JSON schema adherence on structured extraction, and behavior on our prompt caching layer. If those look clean, we will route to it the same way we route to Claude and GPT, with no markup and the same Naira billing.

We will post the evaluation results here when we have them. Subscribe via RSS if you want to see them the day they land.
